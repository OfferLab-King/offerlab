# OfferLab product strategy and roadmap

**Status:** Approved product direction
**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Last reviewed:** 2026-08-07
**Authority:** Defines product positioning, differentiation and priority. Read with `experience-principles.md`, which governs how each feature should behave.

> **Current implementation boundary:** See `current-product-contract.md`. Delivery phases express validation order; they are not evidence that an already implemented pilot is unavailable, nor approval to add payments, coach access or marketplace infrastructure without the required decisions.

## Product thesis

OfferLab helps graduates become more employable and perform better in recruitment by combining four forms of value:

1. **Prepare:** organise applications, tailor truthful CVs and cover letters, and turn genuine
   experience into reusable interview evidence.
2. **Learn:** study concise skills, frameworks and annotated examples grounded in graduate recruitment.
3. **Practise:** rehearse interviews, case work and group exercises that cannot be learned through reading alone.
4. **Connect:** access current candidate intelligence and, when useful, vetted peers or coaches.

The application tracker, Answer Bank and resource library are the dependable workspace foundation. They create continuity and personal context. They are not, by themselves, the complete differentiation.

## Distinctive reasons to join

OfferLab should earn attention by demonstrating a small number of concrete, hard-to-reproduce benefits:

- **Annotated coaching cases:** original anonymised work, precise coach comments, revisions and the reasoning behind a stronger result.
- **Current recruitment intelligence:** moderated, cycle-dated reports about formats, themes, assessed skills and candidate reflections without confidential materials.
- **Group Mock:** realistic group-exercise practice, initially delivered as scheduled peer or facilitator-led sessions rather than automated matching.
- **Answer and Story Bank:** a member-owned store of real evidence that can support multiple answers and applications.
- **Targeted application documents:** private CV and cover-letter version histories that help a
  member connect genuine evidence to one role, company and job description without losing a
  reusable base version.
- **Curated common questions:** focused top-question collections by question family, stage and, where editorial evidence supports it, industry or role context.
- **Industry and business cases:** case prompts grouped by industry and topic, with analysis frameworks, worked reasoning and practice variants.
- **Professional language and skills:** practical communication, group-exercise and workplace skills taught in context, not a generic course catalogue.
- **Premium feedback:** bounded review of answers, CVs, written exercises or mock interviews by a vetted coach; a curated panel precedes any open marketplace.

These benefits should be visible in public positioning and easy to discover after sign-in. Workspace-first UX means direct and compact interaction; it does not mean hiding OfferLab's distinctive content and services.

## Attention hierarchy

Do not give every capability equal visual weight. Use limited attention in this order:

1. State a concrete outcome relevant to the member's immediate recruitment problem.
2. Demonstrate one distinctive OfferLab artefact or experience.
3. Offer one specific action.
4. Keep supporting workspace tools easy to reach without competing with the primary message.

Public pages should lead with evidence of value: an annotated coaching excerpt, a sample current-intelligence record, a Group Mock scenario or a story reused across answers. Only present a feature as available when it can be used or booked; otherwise use an honest pilot or wait-list label. Avoid leading with a long feature list or abstract “career operating system” language.

The signed-in experience should separate:

- **My workspace:** Jobs, Applications, CVs, Cover letters and Answer Bank.
- **Explore:** Questions, annotated cases, industry cases, skills and resources.
- **Practise and get feedback:** Group Mock and premium review services.
- **Intelligence:** current moderated recruitment reports.

This is an information hierarchy, not a requirement for four large dashboards or a fixed journey. Navigation should remain compact and may combine destinations when user testing supports it.

## Product definitions

### Annotated coaching cases

Each case should show the original anonymised material, the intended competency or outcome, specific problems, coach questions or comments, revisions and why the final version is stronger. Cases may cover answers, CV statements, written exercises, presentations and group-exercise behaviour. The coaching reasoning is the premium value; the final answer alone is not.

### Common questions

Offer a deliberately curated set rather than an inflated catalogue. “Top 10” or “Top 20” collections are editorial products, not universal statistical claims unless supported by evidence. Members can browse by family, recruitment stage and supported industry or role context, then draft an answer directly.

### Industry cases and case analysis

Keep these distinct from annotated student-answer cases. Industry cases teach how to structure and analyse a business situation. Organise them by industry, case topic and framework, while explaining when a framework is useful and where rigid application fails. Include worked reasoning and a practice version.

### Group Mock

Group Mock solves a problem members cannot reproduce alone. Validate it first as a manually scheduled service with clear session format, availability, participant expectations, safeguarding and feedback. Add automatic matching only after recurring demand and sufficient concurrent supply are demonstrated.

### Premium feedback and coaching

Start with a small vetted coach panel and clearly bounded services, prices, turnaround times and outputs. Examples include answer review, interview mock, CV review and written-exercise feedback. Do not build an open tutor marketplace until quality control, scheduling, payments, refunds, safeguarding and supply-demand balance are proven operationally.

### Community intelligence

The primary product is a searchable, moderated report database, not a chat feed. Reports should show recruitment cycle, approximate date, stage, format, general themes, assessed skills, reflections and moderation confidence. Prohibit confidential documents, exact restricted questions, personal data and copyrighted assessment material. Discussion is supporting context, not the system of record.

## Delivery sequence

### Foundation — available or in progress

- Open member registration and trusted account access.
- Applications and deadlines.
- Private saved job targets, with manual entry always available and optional provider discovery.
- Multiple CV and cover-letter versions with role-specific targeting and bounded review.
- Answer and Story Bank.
- Common-question catalogue foundation.
- Resource library and optional Preparation Plans.
- Deterministic, secondary recommendations.

### Phase 1 — demonstrate proprietary value

1. Publish a small founder-reviewed collection of annotated coaching cases.
2. Curate the first Top Questions collections and connect them directly to Answer Bank drafting.
3. Pilot structured recruitment reports with moderation, confidentiality controls and clearly dated provenance.
4. Run the first Group Mock sessions as a manually scheduled pilot.
5. Offer one or two bounded founder or vetted-coach feedback services.
6. Rework public positioning so visitors can preview these formats and see honest availability before registration.
7. Evaluate and prototype the bounded Answer Coach defined in `ai-product-strategy.md`, using Story Bank evidence and a founder-reviewed rubric before any member pilot.

This phase tests whether members value OfferLab's judgement, current intelligence and live support, not merely its organisational tools. It does not require automatic matching, an open marketplace or a full social network.

### Phase 2 — productise proven practice and skills

1. Improve Group Mock scheduling, participant preparation and feedback based on pilot evidence.
2. Expand only the feedback services with demonstrated demand and reliable delivery.
3. Add professional-language content for group exercises and workplace communication.
4. Publish a small library of industry cases and worked analysis.

Operational delivery may use simple external scheduling and video tools before bespoke marketplace software is justified.

### Phase 3 — deepen skills and network effects

- Expand contextual skill labs only where demand is demonstrated.
- Add corroboration and contributor recognition to recruitment intelligence.
- Improve peer matching after session liquidity is proven.
- Expand only the AI coaching and editorial tasks that have met their usefulness, safety, privacy and unit-economics gates; retain clear separation from human coach feedback.

### Phase 4 — experience creation

- Curated projects, charity partnerships and skills sprints that create genuine evidence.
- Employer or partner challenges with defined deliverables and supervision.
- An evidence portfolio that feeds completed work back into the Story Bank.

Do not begin with a broad volunteering marketplace, multi-city events operation or pay-to-intern positioning.

## Commercial model to test

- **Public proof:** selected videos, articles, question examples and partial annotated cases.
- **Membership:** workspace, full premium cases, question collections, resources and recruitment intelligence.
- **Paid services:** Group Mock, coach feedback and mock interviews, sold as clear one-off services or credits.

Do not require the full marketplace, community or payment vision to exist before testing willingness to pay. A manually operated service is acceptable while demand is uncertain.

## Return behaviour

OfferLab should aim for timely repeat use, not artificial daily engagement. Natural return triggers include a newly found role, a document that needs tailoring, a deadline, stage change, interview invitation, new relevant intelligence, scheduled practice, feedback delivery and post-interview reflection. Notifications must be opt-in, concise and tied to one useful action.

## Evidence required before expansion

Prioritisation should follow observed behaviour and interviews. Track privacy-safe evidence such as:

- first useful artefact created;
- a base CV or cover letter turned into a truthful role-specific version;
- a saved job target reused while preparing an application document;
- a bounded document review leading to an explicit member edit rather than an automatic rewrite;
- a story reused in an answer;
- a question opened and answer drafted;
- an annotated case viewed deeply enough to reach the reasoning;
- a recruitment report search producing a useful result;
- Group Mock booking, attendance and repeat intent;
- premium feedback purchase and satisfaction;
- return following a real application event.

Do not use page count, catalogue size, completion percentage or daily activity as proof of value by themselves.
