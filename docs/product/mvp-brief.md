**Document version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft source of truth

## 1. Product summary

### Product name

OfferLab

### Product category

Graduate employability and recruitment preparation platform.

### One-sentence description

OfferLab helps students understand what to prepare, develop stronger employability skills, and learn from recent candidate experiences throughout the UK graduate recruitment process.

### Initial product promise

> Know what to prepare, learn how to prepare, and understand what candidates are experiencing now.

---

## 2. Vision

OfferLab exists to help graduates become genuinely more employable, rather than merely teaching them how to perform interview techniques.

The platform should help students:

- Understand graduate recruitment processes.
- Identify what they need to prepare next.
- Develop professional and employability skills.
- Build stronger evidence and interview examples.
- Learn from real candidate experiences.
- Prepare in a structured and accountable way.
- Receive additional human or AI support when required.

The long-term vision is to become a career operating system for graduates.

The MVP will validate one narrower proposition:

> Students will pay for structured recruitment preparation that combines expert coaching content, application planning and current community intelligence.

---

## 3. Product principles

### 3.1 Employability before interview tricks

OfferLab should teach skills and behaviours students can genuinely use in projects, internships, part-time work, volunteering and future employment.

### 3.2 Build genuine evidence

Students should be encouraged to create real examples rather than manufacture stories for interviews.

### 3.3 Guidance before information overload

The platform should help users decide what to do next instead of presenting an unstructured library of resources.

### 3.4 Structured workflows before unrestricted AI

Rules, templates, guided questions and predefined pathways should handle most user needs.

AI should only be used where personalised interpretation creates meaningful value.

### 3.5 Community knowledge should be structured

Candidate experiences should become searchable and reusable intelligence rather than disappearing inside group chats.

### 3.6 Explain why

OfferLab should not only provide model answers.

It should explain:

- Why an answer is weak.
- Why a phrase creates concern.
- Why an employer asks a question.
- Why an improved answer is stronger.
- Why a certain preparation activity matters.

### 3.7 Current information matters

Recruitment reports should clearly show the relevant company, role, stage and recruitment cycle.

### 3.8 Trust and confidentiality matter

OfferLab should not encourage users to share confidential documents, restricted assessment materials or personally identifiable information.

## 4. Initial target user

### Primary user

A university student or recent graduate applying for UK graduate schemes or internships.

### Typical profile

- Based in the UK or applying to UK employers.
- Undergraduate, postgraduate or recent graduate.
- Applying for competitive graduate roles or internships.
- Has limited understanding of professional recruitment.
- May be an international student.
- Has difficulty turning existing experiences into strong examples.
- Uses YouTube, Xiaohongshu, university resources, ChatGPT and peer groups for preparation.
- Is willing to pay for greater structure, confidence and current information.

### Common target industries

- Consulting.
- Financial services.
- Accounting and professional services.
- Technology.
- Public sector.
- Consumer and retail graduate programmes.
- General corporate graduate schemes.

The MVP does not need to serve every industry equally. Initial content can focus on the sectors where OfferLab has the strongest expertise and existing student evidence.

---

## 5. Secondary users

Secondary users may include:

- Recent graduates reapplying after unsuccessful recruitment cycles.
- International students seeking help with UK professional communication.
- Students preparing for assessment centres.
- Students who need structured accountability.
- Early-career professionals applying for their next role.

These users may use the MVP, but the initial product should not be designed around all of them.

## 6. Core user problems

### Problem 1: Students do not know what to prepare next

Recruitment advice is scattered across videos, PDFs, group chats, forums and company websites.

Students struggle to decide:

- What matters for their current application stage.
- What to prioritise.
- What can wait.
- Which resources are relevant.
- How to organise multiple applications.

### Problem 2: Existing content is generic

Much of the available advice teaches broad techniques such as STAR but does not explain:

- How employers assess answers.
- Why specific answers fail.
- How to improve an example.
- What strong candidates do differently.
- How workplace behaviours relate to recruitment exercises.

### Problem 3: Candidate experience information is poor

Students want to know what other candidates have experienced for a particular company, role and recruitment cycle.

Existing sources are often:

- Outdated.
- Too brief.
- Unstructured.
- Difficult to search.
- Unverified.
- Dominated by status discussions rather than useful preparation advice.

### Problem 4: Students struggle to communicate their experiences

Many students have potentially useful examples but cannot clearly explain:

- Their personal contribution.
- The challenge.
- Their reasoning.
- The result.
- What they learned.
- Why the experience is relevant.

### Problem 5: Students prepare reactively

Many begin serious preparation only when an interview or assessment centre is close.

They do not have a single place to manage applications, preparation and progress.

## 7. Jobs to be done

When I am applying for graduate roles, I want OfferLab to help me:

1. Understand what stage I am at.
2. Decide what I need to prepare next.
3. Track my applications and deadlines.
4. Learn how employers assess candidates.
5. See examples of weak and strong answers.
6. Understand why an answer works.
7. Prepare for specific recruitment exercises.
8. Learn what candidates have recently experienced.
9. Save useful resources and continue where I stopped.
10. Contribute my experience to help other members.

---

## 8. MVP value proposition

OfferLab combines three types of value that are usually separated:

### Expert preparation

Structured content based on real coaching experience.

### Personal organisation

Application tracking and stage-based preparation guidance.

### Community intelligence

Recent, structured candidate reports organised by company, role, stage and recruitment cycle.

The MVP does not promise complete career transformation.

It promises a more organised, informed and practical graduate recruitment preparation experience.

## 9. Core MVP user journey

### 9.1 Discovery

The student discovers OfferLab through:

- YouTube.
- Xiaohongshu.
- Search.
- Referral.
- Student society.
- Existing coaching audience.

The content demonstrates one practical insight and directs the user to OfferLab for structured preparation.

### 9.2 Landing page

The landing page explains:

- Who OfferLab is for.
- The main problem it solves.
- What members receive.
- How community reports work.
- What makes OfferLab different from free content and ChatGPT.
- Membership pricing.
- A clear join button.

### 9.3 Registration and payment

The user:

1. Creates an account.
2. Chooses a membership.
3. Completes payment.
4. Accepts community and confidentiality rules.
5. Begins onboarding.

### 9.4 Onboarding

The platform asks:

- University stage.
- Graduate role or internship.
- Target industries.
- Target companies.
- Current recruitment stages.
- Upcoming deadlines.
- Areas of confidence.
- Areas where support is needed.

The MVP can use simple predefined rules rather than AI.

### 9.5 Add first application

The user adds:

- Company.
- Role.
- Location.
- Application deadline.
- Current stage.
- Next known deadline.
- Personal notes.

### 9.6 Receive preparation recommendations

The dashboard recommends relevant actions based on the current stage.

Example:

> You have a video interview in five days.

Recommended actions:

- Complete the video interview preparation path.
- Review teamwork and motivation examples.
- Read recent reports for the company.
- Complete the recording checklist.
- Practise two questions.

### 9.7 Use the knowledge library

The student accesses:

- Learning paths.
- Competency guides.
- Annotated examples.
- Assessment-centre preparation.
- Professional communication resources.
- Downloadable checklists and templates.

### 9.8 Read community reports

The student searches or filters by:

- Company.
- Role.
- Industry.
- Location.
- Recruitment stage.
- Recruitment cycle.
- Report date.

### 9.9 Submit a report

After participating in a recruitment process, the student submits a structured report.

The report enters moderation before publication.

### 9.10 Return

The user returns to:

- Update an application.
- View the next preparation action.
- Continue a learning path.
- Read new reports.
- Check deadlines.
- Submit an experience.
- Review saved content.

## 10. MVP feature scope

## 10.1 Authentication and membership

### Required functionality

- Account registration.
- Login and logout.
- Password reset.
- Email verification.
- Member and administrator roles.
- Stripe subscription payment.
- Subscription status management.
- Cancellation handling.
- Access control for premium content.

### Acceptance criteria

- A new user can register and verify an account.
- A user can purchase a membership.
- Successful payment grants premium access.
- Failed or cancelled payment does not grant premium access.
- A cancelled member retains access until the end of the paid period.
- An administrator can identify active and inactive members.
- Premium pages cannot be accessed without the required membership.

---

## 10.2 Onboarding

### Required functionality

Collect:

- Education or career stage.
- Target opportunity type.
- Target industries.
- Target companies.
- Current recruitment stages.
- Preparation priorities.
- Upcoming deadlines.

### Acceptance criteria

- A new member can complete onboarding in less than five minutes.
- The member can skip non-essential questions.
- Answers are saved to the member profile.
- The member can update answers later.
- The dashboard uses onboarding information to prioritise content.
- No AI API is required for onboarding recommendations.

---

## 10.3 Application tracker

### Required functionality

Members can:

- Add an application.
- Edit an application.
- Delete or archive an application.
- Record company and role.
- Record location.
- Record application stage.
- Record deadlines.
- Add notes.
- Mark an outcome.

### Initial stages

- Planning.
- Application started.
- Application submitted.
- Online test.
- Video interview.
- Telephone interview.
- Assessment centre.
- Final interview.
- Offer.
- Rejected.
- Withdrawn.

### Acceptance criteria

- A member can add an application in under two minutes.
- Applications appear on the member dashboard.
- Applications can be filtered by status.
- Upcoming deadlines are clearly visible.
- Past deadlines are flagged.
- The member can update the recruitment stage.
- Archived applications do not appear in the default active view.
- One member cannot view another member’s application tracker.

---

## 10.4 Stage-based dashboard

### Required functionality

The dashboard should show:

- Active applications.
- Upcoming deadlines.
- Recommended preparation.
- Continue learning.
- Recently viewed content.
- Recent relevant community reports.
- Prompt to contribute after a recruitment event.

### Acceptance criteria

- Dashboard recommendations change when an application stage changes.
- A user with no applications is prompted to add one.
- A user with an upcoming assessment receives relevant preparation links.
- The dashboard does not depend on generative AI.
- Recommendations are based on documented rules.
- Administrators can update recommendation mappings without rebuilding the application where practical.

## 10.6 Flagship learning paths

The MVP should launch with approximately five to ten strong learning paths.

### Recommended initial paths

1. Building a strong teamwork example.
2. Answering motivation questions.
3. Preparing for a video interview.
4. Succeeding in an assessment-centre group exercise.
5. Speaking professionally during group discussions.
6. Preparing for a behavioural interview.
7. Understanding commercial awareness.
8. Using university projects to build employability evidence.
9. Preparing for a written exercise.
10. Managing multiple graduate applications.

### Each learning path should contain

- Learning objective.
- Short lessons.
- Video where useful.
- Framework.
- Examples.
- Common mistakes.
- Practical exercise.
- Reflection or checklist.

### Acceptance criteria

- A member can begin, continue and complete a learning path.

- Each path has a clear practical outcome.

- Each path explains why the recommended behaviour matters.
- Administrators can reorder or update lessons.

## 10.7 Annotated coaching cases

### Required structure

- Anonymised original answer.
- Target question or competency.
- Key weaknesses.
- Sentence-level or section-level commentary.
- Questions the coach would ask.
- Improved answer.
- Explanation of the improvements.
- Practice prompt.

### Acceptance criteria

- No student-identifying information is displayed.
- Each case explains the reasoning behind the changes.
- Cases can be tagged by competency, industry and question type.
- Members can compare original and improved versions.
- Administrators can create and edit cases.
- Cases cannot be downloaded as a complete bulk library through the normal interface.
- Individual pages remain usable on mobile devices.

## 11. Explicit MVP non-goals

The following are not part of the initial MVP.

### Mentor marketplace

No open marketplace where mentors independently bid for or accept student jobs.

### Complex mentor scheduling

No automated multi-mentor availability system.

### Full AI chatbot

No unrestricted career-coaching conversation interface.

### Multiple AI tools

At most, one narrowly scoped experiment may be tested after the core product is functioning.

### Group Mock marketplace

No automated peer-matching and booking system in the initial version.

Group Mocks may be offered manually outside the platform.

### Experience programmes

No OfferLab Projects, charity consulting or managed internships in the initial MVP.

### Volunteering marketplace

No comprehensive volunteering opportunity database or matching engine.

### Offline event management

Events may be tested manually using existing ticketing tools.

### Native mobile application

The MVP will be a responsive web application.

### Large generic skills catalogue

OfferLab will not launch with hundreds of broad Excel, coding or professional courses.

### Unlimited downloads

The core knowledge library will remain inside the platform.

### Full company database

The MVP does not require detailed pages for every UK graduate employer.

### Recruitment question leaks

The product will not position itself as a database of confidential or prohibited exact interview questions.

### University enterprise sales

No complex university licensing or institutional administration is required for MVP launch.

### Employer recruitment platform

OfferLab will not initially process employer applications or act as an applicant tracking system for employers.

## 13. Pricing hypothesis

Pricing should be tested rather than treated as final.

### Recommended initial options

#### Monthly membership

Indicative range:

> £15–£25 per month

Suitable for students with an upcoming recruitment process.

#### Recruitment-season membership

Indicative range:

> £59–£99 for three to six months

This may fit the event-driven nature of graduate recruitment better than a permanent subscription.

#### Founding member offer

Indicative range:

> £39–£69 for limited early access

In exchange for:

- Product feedback.
- Community participation.
- Recruitment report contributions.
- Permission to use anonymised testimonials.

#### Annual membership

Do not prioritise this until retention and seasonality are better understood.

### Recommended first pricing test

Offer two choices:

- £19 per month.
- £69 for six months.

This allows OfferLab to test whether students prefer flexibility or recruitment-cycle access.

The exact figures should be validated with real prospective users before being fixed.

## 16. Success metrics

### Activation metrics

A newly registered member is considered activated when they:

1. Complete onboarding.
2. Add at least one application.
3. Open at least one recommended learning resource.
4. View at least one relevant recruitment report.

#### Initial activation target

At least 50% of paying members complete the activation journey within seven days.

### Engagement metrics

Track:

- Weekly active paid members.
- Applications updated.
- Learning paths started.
- Learning paths completed.
- Reports viewed.
- Saved resources.
- Community participation.
- Return visits.

#### Initial engagement target

At least 40% of paying members should return in a second week.

### Content value metrics

Track:

- Most viewed content.
- Completion rates.
- Saves.
- User ratings.
- Content-related support requests.
- Learning-path abandonment.

#### Initial content target

At least 30% of members should complete one learning path within their first month.

### Community intelligence metrics

Track:

- Reports submitted.
- Approval rate.
- Report completeness.
- Reports viewed.
- Companies and roles covered.
- Percentage relating to the current recruitment cycle.

#### Initial contribution target

At least 10% of active members should submit one usable recruitment report during their membership period.

### Commercial metrics

Track:

- Visitor-to-registration conversion.
- Registration-to-paid conversion.
- Monthly recurring revenue.
- Recruitment-season package sales.
- Refunds.
- Cancellation rate.
- Acquisition source.
- Cost to acquire a paid member where applicable.

### Qualitative success criteria

Members should report that OfferLab helps them:

- Feel less overwhelmed.
- Know what to prepare next.
- Understand their mistakes.
- Prepare more efficiently.
- Access information they could not easily find elsewhere.
- Feel more confident in recruitment situations.

## 17. MVP validation questions

The MVP exists to answer the following business questions:

1. Will students pay for structured access rather than individual PDFs or courses?
2. Is current community intelligence a strong enough reason to join?
3. Does an application tracker increase repeat usage?
4. Which content categories create the greatest value?
5. Do members complete learning paths or mainly consume individual resources?
6. Will members contribute useful recruitment reports?
7. Which membership duration best matches graduate recruitment behaviour?
8. Do students return after the immediate interview or assessment?
9. Is OfferLab meaningfully better than using YouTube, ChatGPT and free online forums separately?
10. Which premium service should be built next?

## 18. Risks and mitigations

### Risk: Too much scope

**Mitigation:** Use the explicit non-goals list. New ideas remain in the roadmap until the MVP is validated.

### Risk: Empty community

**Mitigation:** Recruit founding members manually and seed discussions before public launch.

### Risk: Insufficient recruitment reports

**Mitigation:** Invite past students to contribute appropriately anonymised experiences and reward approved future contributions.

### Risk: Low-quality or unreliable reports

**Mitigation:** Use structured forms, moderation, dates, recruitment cycles and confidence labels.

### Risk: Confidential information is shared

**Mitigation:** Use clear submission warnings, mandatory confirmation and active moderation.

### Risk: Students join only for one report and leave

**Mitigation:** Connect reports to application tracking, preparation pathways, examples and progress tools.

### Risk: Content is copied or shared

**Mitigation:** Keep the main experience online, update it regularly and make the value come from structure, explanation, community freshness and progress tracking.

### Risk: Product becomes another course library

**Mitigation:** Make the dashboard and recommended next actions central to the user experience.

### Risk: The application tracker feels generic

**Mitigation:** Connect each application stage directly to OfferLab preparation content and current reports.

### Risk: Building takes too long

**Mitigation:** Use existing services for authentication, payments, email, analytics and community functionality where appropriate.

### Risk: Founder capacity

**Mitigation:** Keep operations manually manageable and do not launch mentor, project or event marketplaces initially.

## 23. Product roadmap after MVP

The next feature should be selected based on observed behaviour.

### If members need live practice

Build Group Mock scheduling and peer matching.

### If members struggle to form examples

Build the guided competency-answer builder.

### If international students show strong demand

Expand the Professional Language Library.

### If users request individual feedback

Introduce one bounded AI feedback tool or curated mentor review.

### If members want human support

Launch a small curated mentor panel.

### If students lack genuine experience

Pilot one OfferLab Project with a charity or partner organisation.

No major second module should be built only because it sounds attractive.

## 25. Final MVP statement

OfferLab MVP is a paid, responsive web platform for UK graduate applicants.

It combines:

- An application tracker.
- Stage-based preparation recommendations.
- Expert learning content.
- Annotated coaching cases.
- Searchable current recruitment reports.
- A controlled member community.

It deliberately excludes marketplaces, complex AI, managed experience programmes and large-scale offline operations.

Its purpose is to validate whether students will repeatedly pay for a more structured, practical and current graduate recruitment preparation experience.
