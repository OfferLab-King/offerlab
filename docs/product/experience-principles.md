# OfferLab product experience principles

**Status:** Approved and current
**Date:** 2026-07-23
**Authority:** Governs product, design and implementation decisions. It supersedes guided-journey defaults in earlier draft product documents.

This document governs interaction design. `product-strategy-and-roadmap.md` governs what OfferLab should make prominent and build next. A workspace-first interface must still demonstrate OfferLab's distinctive coaching cases, current intelligence, practice and feedback services.

## Foundational principle

OfferLab is a preparation workspace, not a course or wizard.

Give members clean structures for applications, evidence, answers and resources. Explain unfamiliar concepts briefly once, then let members work directly. Do not assume members need step-by-step guidance for ordinary actions. Add guidance only where misunderstanding would cause a meaningful error.

## Defaults

- **Workspace over course.** Members track applications, save evidence stories, prepare answers, browse questions, use resources and optionally follow Preparation Plans. OfferLab is not a mandatory curriculum, gamified completion system, wizard or fixed sequence.
- **Explain once.** Prefer clear terminology, one-sentence descriptions, short contextual hints and useful examples over repeated explanations, tutorials or large instructional panels.
- **Direct manipulation.** Let members add, edit, save, archive, restore, filter, link and reorder directly. Prefer one-page forms. Use multiple steps only for genuine dependencies, unmanageable complexity, safety or privacy confirmation, or demonstrated user need.
- **User autonomy.** Members may create answers before stories, keep unused stories, prepare selected questions, skip irrelevant families and work in the order their applications require. Recommendations and Preparation Plans are optional.
- **Minimal guidance.** Use clear titles, labels, status text, specific actions and proportionate empty states. Do not default to tours, forced sequencing, estimated times, artificial targets, repeated “how it works” content or multiple progress indicators.
- **Decision-supporting metrics.** Show a count, gap, coverage or progress measure only when it helps a member make a decision. Avoid empty coverage grids, `0 of 0`, arbitrary percentages, preparation scores and compulsory-looking targets.
- **Compact functional UI.** Prefer compact navigation, sensible density, restrained headings, compact rows or cards, visible primary actions, clear statuses and responsive layouts. Ordinary CRUD pages should not resemble marketing landing pages.
- **Specific actions.** Prefer labels such as “Add story”, “Edit story”, “Use in an answer”, “Save draft”, “Mark ready” and “Browse questions” over “Continue journey” or “Take the next step” when the real action is known.

## Product-area definitions

### Answer Bank

The Answer Bank is a workspace where members save reusable evidence stories, prepare answers to interview questions and browse common questions. A story may support multiple answers, and an answer may use one or more stories.

Its primary sections are Stories, Answers and Questions. An overview is optional and should exist only when it provides practical value. Coverage and recommended actions are supporting information, not the main structure. Do not force a story → competency → answer → coverage sequence.

### Preparation Plans

Preparation Plans are optional structured checklists for members who want broader stage-specific preparation. They are not the mandatory navigation model. Members can use the Answer Bank, Applications and Resources without starting or following a plan.

### Recommendations

Recommendations may be deterministic, explainable and concise, but must remain secondary to direct navigation and easy to ignore or dismiss. Do not redesign each page around a next recommended action.

## Proportionate guidance and future scope

Retain necessary guidance for authentication, verification, privacy, security, destructive actions, ownership, readiness validation, accessibility and genuinely unfamiliar concepts. Keep useful field guidance for Situation, Task, Actions, Reasoning, Result and Reflection.

Future briefs start with the simplest direct workflow. Add guidance only after demonstrated need; do not create dashboards merely because aggregate data exists. Coaching Cases, Practice Lab, Recruitment Intelligence and Expert Feedback must establish their core direct workflow before any elaborate guided journey is proposed.
